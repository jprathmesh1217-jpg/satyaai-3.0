# Courier, Contraband & Digital Arrest Scams

## Overview
Courier scams, often escalating into "Digital Arrest" fraud, occur when scammers impersonate logistics operators (FedEx, DHL, India Post) and law enforcement agencies (CBI, Mumbai Police, Narcotics Control Bureau) to extort massive sums under psychological intimidation.

## The Modus Operandi
1. **The Automated Robocall**:
   - Victim receives an automated call: "Your FedEx parcel containing illegal contraband (passports, MDMA, narcotics) has been intercepted at customs. Press 1 to connect to an officer."
2. **The Fake Police Transfer**:
   - The call is transferred to a fraudster wearing a police uniform in front of a synthetic police station backdrop on Skype or WhatsApp Video.
3. **The "Digital Arrest" Threat**:
   - Scammers claim an arrest warrant has been issued in the victim's name for money laundering (referencing real cases such as Jet Airways or illegal hawala networks).
   - They place the victim under "Digital Arrest", ordering them to remain on camera 24/7 in an isolated room without contacting anyone.
4. **The "RBI Safe Verification Account" Extortion**:
   - Scammers demand that the victim transfer their entire savings to an "RBI audit escrow account" to prove their funds are legitimate, promising the money will be refunded in 30 minutes after clearance. Once transferred, the scammers vanish.

## Red Flags
- There is **NO LEGAL CONCEPT** of "Digital Arrest" in Indian law or international jurisprudence. Police and agencies never arrest individuals over video calls.
- Police, CBI, ED, and NCB never demand money or fund transfers to clear your name or verify accounts.
- Extreme coercion demanding that you do not disconnect the video call or inform family members.

## Immediate Action
- **Hang up immediately**. Disconnecting the call carries zero legal consequence.
- Immediately contact the nearest local police station or dial **1930** (Cyber Crime Helpline).
- File a formal cybercrime report at `https://cybercrime.gov.in`.
