# Authority & Executive Impersonation Scams

## Overview
Impersonation fraud relies on false authority, where attackers pose as legitimate institutional figures—such as bank managers, telecom regulators (TRAI), police officials, or company executives (CEO fraud / Business Email Compromise)—to compel compliance.

## Pretexts & Techniques
1. **Utility & Telecom Impersonation**:
   - "Electricity Officer: Your power will be disconnected at 9:30 PM tonight due to an unpaid bill. Call officer Sharma at 9876543210 immediately."
   - "TRAI Notification: All mobile numbers registered under your Aadhaar will be disconnected in 2 hours due to illegal broadcasting."
2. **Business Email Compromise (BEC) / CEO Fraud**:
   - Attackers spoof or compromise senior executive email accounts.
   - An urgent, confidential message instructs the finance team or an employee to purchase gift cards or process an urgent wire transfer to an unknown vendor.
3. **Government Agency Impersonation**:
   - Displaying fabricated digital letterheads, fake Supreme Court arrest warrants, or forged RBI letters stamped with official-looking emblems.

## Key Indicators
- Unofficial contact channels (Gmail addresses for corporate leaders, personal cell phone numbers for utility officers).
- Demands for immediate non-standard payment methods: UPI transfers, gift cards, or cryptocurrency.
- High consequences threatened for questioning authority or seeking verification.

## Defense Strategy
- Validate claims through known, established internal or public telephone directories, never by calling back the number that contacted you.
- For business wire transfers, implement dual-person out-of-band authorization protocols (e.g., verbal in-person confirmation).
